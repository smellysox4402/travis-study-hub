THE INGREDIENT SHELF 🍽

Every dish in the organic kitchen is built from carbon. The ingredient shelf is where we sort the ingredients: what a formula tells you, what a functional group is, and how families of compounds line up in homologous series.

S3.2.1 — The recipe card: four ways to write a formula

📄 EMPIRICAL

Simplest whole-number ratio of atoms. Glucose C6H12O6 → CH2O. No structural info.

🔢 MOLECULAR

Actual number of atoms: C6H12O6. Still no structure.

🖼 STRUCTURAL

Three flavours: full/displayed (all bonds), condensed (CH3CH2CH3), skeletal (vertices = carbons, H hidden, functional atoms shown).

Carbon: 4 valence electrons → 4 bonds, and catenation (chains of identical atoms) → straight, branched or cyclic. That is why carbon chemistry is so vast.

S3.2.2 — The ingredient shelf: functional groups

A functional group is the atom or group of atoms that decides a compound's physical and chemical character. Same group → same class → similar reactions. R stands for any carbon-containing (alkyl) group.

Saturated = all C–C single bonds (alkanes). Unsaturated = at least one C=C or C≡C (alkenes, alkynes). Aliphatic = no aromatic ring.

S3.2.3 — Recipe families: homologous series

A homologous series is a family with the same functional group and a shared general formula; each member differs from the next by one CH2.

SAME DISH, DIFFERENT PLATE 🍳

Bigger chains boil hotter, names follow strict rules, and the same ingredients can be arranged into completely different dishes: isomers.

S3.2.4 — Bigger chain, hotter pot

Boiling point climbs steadily along a homologous series: each added CH2 strengthens London (dispersion) forces. Methane −161 °C → ethane −89 → propane −42 → butane −0.5 → pentane 36 → hexane 69 °C. Melting point is noisier — don't rely on it for prediction. Density and viscosity also rise with chain length.

S3.2.5 — The naming rules (IUPAC)

🌱 ROOTS

1 meth- · 2 eth- · 3 prop- · 4 but- · 5 pent- · 6 hex-

🧱 STEPS

Longest chain → root + -ane/-ene/-yne → number to give substituents lowest locants → prefixes in alphabetical order (3-ethyl-2-methylhexane) → multipliers for repeats (2,3-dimethylhexane).

🎯 PRIORITY

C=C gets lowest locant over substituents (4-methylhex-2-ene). –OH over C=C (propan-2-ol). Carbonyl lowest locant. –COOH always terminal, no locant.

Alkenes: but-1-ene, 4,5-dimethylhex-2-ene. Halogenoalkanes: 2-chloro-1,1,1-trifluoroethane; C=C beats halogen (3-chlorobut-1-ene). Aldehydes: methanal, 4-methylpentanal, 3-fluorobutanal. Ketones: propanone, butanone, 4-methylpentan-2-one. Acids: 3-bromopropanoic acid, 4-methylpentanoic acid.

S3.2.6 — Structural isomers: same formula, different skeleton

🔗 CHAIN

Different carbon skeleton: butane vs methylpropane (both C4H10).

📍 POSITIONAL

Functional group moves: 1-, 2-, 3-bromopentane (C5H11Br).

🍳 FUNCTIONAL GROUP

Same atoms, new group: ethanol vs methoxymethane (C2H6O); propanal vs propanone (C3H6O); methyl methanoate vs ethanoic acid (C2H4O2).

1°, 2°, 3° = number of carbons bonded to the functional-group carbon. For amines count C bonded to N directly (N-methylpropan-1-amine). C4H9F has exactly 4 structural isomers: 1-fluoro-, 2-fluoro-, 1-fluoro-2-methylpropane, 2-fluoro-2-methylpropane.

S3.2.7 (AHL) — Stereoisomers: same skeleton, different 3D pose

↔ CIS-TRANS

C=C blocks rotation. cis = identical groups same side; trans = opposite sides. cis-/trans-but-2-ene. Propene: no (one C has two identical H). Cycloalkanes too: 1,2-dimethylcyclobutane.

🪞 OPTICAL

Chiral carbon = 4 different groups. Enantiomers = non-superimposable mirror images; rotate plane-polarised light +/− same angle. Racemic 50:50 mix: no rotation. Wedge = toward you, dash = away.

Configurational isomers need bond-breaking to interconvert; conformational isomers (staggered vs eclipsed ethane) just rotate about single bonds — can't be separated.

THE KITCHEN SCANNER 🔬

Can't see the molecule? Scan it. Mass spec weighs the pieces, IR feels the bonds, NMR counts the hydrogen neighbourhoods. Together they identify the dish.

S3.2.8 — Mass spectrometry: weigh the fragments

MS smashes the molecule into charged fragments. Highest m/z = the molecular ion M⁺ = relative molecular mass. Fragment masses hint at pieces: 15 = CH3⁺, 17 = OH⁺, 29 = CHO⁺ or CH3CH2⁺, 31 = CH3O⁺ or CH2OH⁺, 45 = COOH⁺.

Propan-1-ol (Mr 60): m/z 60 = M⁺, 31 = [CH2OH]⁺ (loss of ethyl), 29 = [CH3CH2]⁺.

S3.2.9 — IR: feel the bonds

Bonds vibrate; IR-active bonds (changing dipole) absorb at characteristic wavenumbers. C=O ~1700–1750 cm⁻¹ (strong), carboxylic acid O–H ~2500–3000 cm⁻¹ (very broad). Vibration frequency rises with bond enthalpy, falls with atomic mass (HCl 2886 > HBr 2559 > HI 2230 cm⁻¹). Homonuclear diatomics (H2, O2, Cl2): IR-inactive — no dipole change.

S3.2.10 + 3.2.11 — ¹H NMR: count the hydrogen neighbourhoods

📊 SIGNALS

One signal per distinct H environment. Integration (area) = relative H count. Propanone: 1 signal. Ethanoic acid: 2 (COOH ~9–13 ppm, CH3 ~2 ppm, ratio 1:3). TMS = 0 ppm reference.

🔀 SPLITTING (N+1)

A proton with N neighbours splits into N+1 peaks: 0 → singlet, 1 → doublet, 2 → triplet (1:2:1), 3 → quartet (1:3:3:1). 1,1,2-trichloroethane: Ha doublet (2H), Hb triplet (1H).

Butanone: A CH3 next to C=O — singlet 2.1 ppm; B CH2 — quartet 2.4; C terminal CH3 — triplet 1.1. 1,1-dichloroethane: CH3 doublet 2.0 + CH quartet 5.8, ratio 3:1.

S3.2.12 — Combine everything: one unknown, one answer

Worked example 11: chlorination of ethane gives X. Combustion: 24.27% C, 4.08% H, 71.65% Cl → mol ratio 2.021 : 4.04 : 2.021 → empirical C2H4Cl2 (Mr 99). Isotope pattern (³⁵Cl/³⁷Cl) gives peaks 98, 100, 63, 65. ¹H NMR: 2 signals, 3:1, doublet at 2.0 + quartet at 5.8 → 1,1-dichloroethane CH3CHCl2.

Worked example 8: 62.02% C, 10.43% H, 27.55% O → C3H6O (Mr 58); IR C=O at 1700–1750; M⁺ = 58; not terminal C=O → propanone.

THE FREE-SPIRIT CHEFS 🔥

Some chefs work alone with a single electron. Radicals are neutral species with one unpaired electron — formed by homolytic fission, they run the kitchen as a chain reaction.

R3.3.1 — The radical: one unpaired electron

A radical is a chemical entity with an unpaired electron, drawn with a dot: Cl•, •CH3, •OH. Unlike ions, radicals exist independently — no counter-ion needed. High reactivity means radicals are usually intermediates, not final products. Radicals are blamed in the free-radical theory of ageing (mitochondria, oxidative stress — still debated).

R3.3.2 — Homolytic fission: splitting the pair

Homolytic fission = the two electrons of a covalent bond split evenly, one to each atom: Cl2 → 2 Cl• (UV light or heat required). Drawn with single-barbed fish-hook arrows (one electron per arrow). This is the initiation step — the spark of the chain. Curly-arrow rules: start at the electrons' origin, end at their destination, rich → poor.

R3.3.3 — Radical substitution: the three-stage chain

Alkanes are inert (C–C 346, C–H 414 kJ mol⁻¹, non-polar). Chlorination in UV light fixes that:

⚡ INITIATION

Cl2 → 2 Cl• (homolytic fission, UV/heat)

🔁 PROPAGATION ×2

Cl• + CH4 → HCl + •CH3; then •CH3 + Cl2 → CH3Cl + Cl•. Cycle repeats: radical + molecule → molecule + new radical.

🛑 TERMINATION

Radical + radical → molecule: Cl•+Cl• → Cl2; •CH3+Cl• → CH3Cl; •CH3+•CH3 → C2H6 (by-product).

F2 too reactive (smashes C–C bonds), I2 too sluggish — so radical halogenation works with Cl2/Br2 only. Chloromethane's polar C–Cl bond makes it a useful stepping stone for further reactions.

THE PARTNER EXCHANGE 🕺

Nucleophiles (electron-rich, donate pairs) meet electrophiles (electron-hungry, accept pairs). The dance has names: SN1, SN2, electrophilic addition — and for AHL, Lewis theory and benzene.

R3.4.1–3.4.4 — The dancers and the fission

🕺 NUCLEOPHILE

Electron-rich, lone pair, donates a pair: OH⁻, CN⁻, Cl⁻, H2O, NH3. (CH3)4N⁺ can't — no lone pair.

💃 ELECTROPHILE

Electron-deficient, accepts a pair: cations (CH3⁺), δ+ atoms (BF3, carbonyl C).

✂️ HETEROLYTIC FISSION

Both electrons to one atom: CH3Br → CH3⁺ + Br⁻ (double-barbed arrow to Br). Makes carbocations.

Nucleophilic substitution: Nu⁻ + R–X → R–Nu + X⁻. Chloroethane + OH⁻ → ethanol + Cl⁻. The leaving group takes the electron pair.

R3.4.5 — Alkenes: electrophilic addition (SL)

The C=C double bond is electron-rich → attacked by electrophiles. Halogens: ethene + Br2 → 1,2-dibromoethane (bromine water decolourises = test for unsaturation). H–X: but-2-ene + HBr → 2-bromobutane. Water (acid-catalysed): hex-3-ene → hexan-3-ol (hydration; proton consumed then regenerated = catalyst).

R3.4.6–3.4.8 (AHL) — Lewis theory: electron-pair economy

Lewis acid = electron-pair acceptor; Lewis base = electron-pair donor. Bigger than Brønsted–Lowry — covers reactions with no proton. BF3 + NH3 → F3B–NH3: NH3 is Lewis base/nucleophile, BF3 is Lewis acid/electrophile. AlCl3 dimerises to Al2Cl6 (both roles at once). All Lewis bases are nucleophiles, all Lewis acids are electrophiles — and vice versa. Transition metal cations = Lewis acids; ligands (H2O, NH3, CN⁻, Cl⁻, OH⁻) donate pairs → complex ions: [Co(H2O)6]²⁺ ⇌ [CoCl4]²⁻ (pink ⇌ blue). Charge math: overall = metal charge + Σ(ligand charge).

R3.4.9–3.4.10 (AHL) — SN1 vs SN2: two dance styles

⚡ SN2 — ONE STEP

Primary halogenoalkanes. Concerted backside attack (180° from leaving group, umbrella flip = inversion). No intermediate, just a transition state. rate = k[halogenoalkane][nucleophile] — second order. The "2" = two molecules in the slow step.

🐢 SN1 — TWO STEPS

Tertiary halogenoalkanes. Step 1: C–X breaks → carbocation (slow, rate-determining). Step 2: nucleophile attacks the cation. rate = k[halogenoalkane] — first order. Stable carbocations: 3° > 2° > 1° (alkyl groups donate electron density).

Leaving group strength: C–I (228) < C–Br (285) < C–Cl (324) < C–F (492) kJ mol⁻¹ — weaker bond = faster fission = faster reaction. Iodides fastest, fluoroalkanes virtually inert. Rate: 2-iodopropane > 2-bromopropane > 2-chloropropane.

R3.4.11–3.4.12 (AHL) — Addition mechanisms + Markovnikov

Electrophilic addition: (1) electrophile attacks the π bond → carbocation, (2) anion/nucleophile attacks the cation. Markovnikov's rule: the electropositive part (H) bonds to the carbon with the fewest alkyl substituents, so the carbocation forms on the most substituted carbon. HBr + propene → 2-bromopropane (major, via 2° carbocation) vs 1-bromopropane (minor, via 1°).

R3.4.13 (AHL) — Benzene: electrophilic substitution

Benzene's aromatic ring is too stable for addition — it substitutes instead. Nitration: HNO3 + H2SO4 → NO2⁺ (nitronium ion) attacks the π ring → carbocation (aromaticity broken, rate-determining) → H⁺ leaves, aromaticity restored → nitrobenzene.

SELF-TEST ✍

14 questions. Pick an answer — you get instant feedback with the reasoning.

1. Which is the condensed structural formula of propane?

✅ Condensed structural formula shows atoms with bonds implied: CH₃CH₂CH₃. C₃H₈ is the molecular formula.
❌ Condensed formula = atoms in order, bonds implied. CH₃CH₂CH₃.

2. CH₃COCH₂CH₃ belongs to which class?

✅ C=O mid-chain = ketone: butanone. Aldehydes have terminal C=O (butanal = CH₃CH₂CH₂CHO).
❌ Ketone — the carbonyl is mid-chain, not terminal.

3. Butane and methylpropane are an example of...

✅ Same formula C₄H₁₀, different carbon skeleton: chain isomers. Positional = group moves (1-/2-bromobutane). Functional = different group (ethanol vs methoxymethane).
❌ Chain isomers — the skeleton differs: straight butane vs branched methylpropane.

4. Which alkene shows cis-trans isomerism?

✅ But-2-ene: each alkene carbon carries two different groups → cis and trans forms. Propene has CH₂ (two identical H) — no isomerism.
❌ But-2-ene. cis-trans needs each C=C carbon to carry two DIFFERENT groups.

5. (AHL) A chiral carbon is bonded to...

✅ Four different groups → non-superimposable mirror images (enantiomers) → rotates plane-polarised light. Example: 2-aminopropanoic acid (H, CH₃, NH₂, COOH).
❌ Four DIFFERENT groups. Two identical groups = no chirality (e.g. C-3 of penicillamine).

6. (AHL) The mass spectrum of propan-1-ol (Mr 60) shows M⁺ at m/z =

✅ Molecular ion = the intact molecule = Mr = 60. m/z 31 = [CH₂OH]⁺ (lost ethyl); 29 = [CH₃CH₂]⁺.
❌ M⁺ is the highest m/z — the parent molecule (60). Lower peaks are fragments.

7. (AHL) A strong absorption at 1700–1750 cm⁻¹ in IR shows...

✅ C=O stretches at 1700–1750 cm⁻¹. Carboxylic acid O–H is the very broad 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ band.
❌ C=O, 1700–1750 cm⁻¹. That single band nails the carbonyl.

8. (AHL) A CH₃ group next to a CH₂ group (2 neighbours) gives a ¹H NMR signal split into a...

✅ N + 1 rule: 2 neighbours → 3 peaks, ratio 1:2:1. (The CH₂ next to CH₃ shows a quartet — they split each other.)
❌ Triplet (N + 1 = 3). Quartet would need 3 neighbouring protons.

9. Cl₂ → 2 Cl• under UV light is which step?

✅ Initiation: homolytic fission makes the two radicals that start the chain. Termination joins two radicals into a molecule.
❌ Initiation — this is where the chain sparks (homolytic fission, UV or heat).

10. (AHL) The rate equation rate = k[halogenoalkane][nucleophile] describes...

✅ The "2" in SN2 = two molecules in the rate-determining step → second order. SN1 is first order: rate = k[halogenoalkane].
❌ SN2 — both reactants appear in the slow step. SN1's slow step is only the C–X bond breaking.

11. (AHL) Fastest nucleophilic substitution with aqueous NaOH?

✅ C–I bond is weakest (228 kJ mol⁻¹) → fastest heterolytic fission → fastest substitution. C–F (492) is virtually inert.
❌ 2-iodopropane — weakest C–X bond = fastest leaving group departure.

12. (AHL) HBr + propene gives a major product of...

✅ Markovnikov: H bonds to the less-substituted carbon → carbocation forms on the more-substituted carbon (2° > 1° stability) → Br lands on C2.
❌ 2-bromopropane — via the more stable secondary carbocation.

13. (AHL) In BF₃ + NH₃ → F₃B–NH₃, ammonia is a...

✅ NH₃ donates its lone pair to electron-deficient boron → Lewis base (also a nucleophile). BF₃ accepts the pair → Lewis acid. No proton involved, so B–L theory can't describe it.
❌ Lewis base — it donates the electron pair. Lewis acid = acceptor (BF₃).

14. (AHL) The electrophile in benzene nitration is...

✅ HNO₃ + H₂SO₄ generates the nitronium ion NO₂⁺, which attacks the aromatic ring. Rate-determining step = breaking aromaticity.
❌ NO₂⁺ (nitronium ion), made in situ by the acid mixture.
