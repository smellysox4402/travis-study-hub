REDOX — THE ELECTRON GRID

Every reaction is a power exchange: one species loses electrons (oxidized), another gains them (reduced) — never one without the other. R3.2 is the study of that exchange: who's willing to give, who's greedy to take, and how to wire the whole thing into a battery.

Act 1 · R3.2.1 · R3.2.2

THE HANDOVER ⚡

Oxidation and reduction — four definitions, one idea. Learn OIL RIG, then master the half-equation bookkeeping.

🔄

FOUR DEFINITIONS, ONE CONCEPT (R3.2.1)

oxidation: OIL · reduction: RIG

  • Oxygen view: oxidation = gains O (2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO); reduction = loses O (CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O).
  • Hydrogen view: oxidation = loses H; reduction = gains H (C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆, hydrogenation).
  • Electron view (the one IB wants): OIL RIG — Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain of electrons. 2Na → 2Na⁺ + 2e⁻ (oxidized) · Cl₂ + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻ (reduced).
  • Oxidation state view: oxidation state = the charge an atom would have if all bonds were ionic. Oxidation = oxidation state increases; reduction = it decreases. C(s) + 2S(s) → CS₂: C 0 → +4 (oxidized), S 0 → −2 (reduced).
  • Never alone: oxidation without reduction is impossible — they're one reaction viewed from two sides.
  • Disproportionation: one species both reduced AND oxidized — 4KClO₃ → 3KClO₄ + KCl.
  • Oxidizing agent = gets reduced (takes electrons). Reducing agent = gets oxidized (gives electrons).
🧮

HALF-EQUATION BOOKKEEPING (R3.2.2)

the 10-step balancing method

  1. Identify what's oxidized and what's reduced.
  2. Split into oxidation and reduction half-equations.
  3. Balance the atoms being oxidized/reduced.
  4. Oxidation half: electrons on the right, count = change in oxidation state.
  5. Reduction half: electrons on the left, same rule.
  6. Multiply so electrons lost = electrons gained.
  7. Add and cancel electrons.
  8. Acidic: add H₂O to balance O, then H⁺ to balance H.
  9. Basic/neutral: add OH⁻ to balance O, H₂O to balance H.
  10. Check total charge = zero.

Example: 6Fe²⁺ + Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ → 6Fe³⁺ + 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O (Cr +6 → +3, gains 6e⁻; each Fe loses 1e⁻ ×6).

Figure 1 — Electron transfer in 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl. Na loses (oxidized, reducing agent), Cl gains (reduced, oxidizing agent).
MEMORY HOOK: "The reducing agent is the one that REDUCES something else — by giving electrons away. Oxidizing agents are electron thieves."
Act 2 · R3.2.3 · R3.2.4

THE PECKING ORDER 🥊

Who can steal from whom? The activity series + halogen order = the ranking of electron greed.

R3.2.3 · SL

THE ACTIVITY SERIES

Most easily oxidized → least:

K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Sn > Pb > (H) > Cu > Ag > Au > Pt

Higher = better reducing agent, gives electrons eagerly.

Displacement test: Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu (blue fades, red-brown copper appears). Zn is above Cu → Zn gives electrons, Cu²⁺ takes them.

Halogens (oxidizing agents) run the opposite way: reactivity increases UP the group: F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂. Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂ but Cl₂ + 2F⁻ → no reaction.

Group 1 metals: ease of oxidation increases DOWN: Li < Na < K < Rb < Cs.

R3.2.4 · SL

ACID + METAL = HYDROGEN

Reactive metals (above H) + dilute acid → salt + H₂:

Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂

Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂

Zn: 0 → +2 (oxidized, reducing agent) · H: +1 → 0 (reduced, acid is the oxidizing agent).

Pop test: collect gas, lit splint → small explosion confirms H₂.

Cu, Ag, Au are below H → no reaction with dilute acids. That's why gold sits elemental in streams and lithium is impossible to pan.

Figure 2 — Activity series (Fig 7). Above H: react with acids. Below H: too lazy to give electrons. Halogens rank opposite.
MEMORY HOOK: "Kali Never Can Manage Anything, Zinc Fights Snappily, Please (H)urry Copper And Gold Plates." Read it top-to-bottom = reactivity order.

THE GRID 🔋

Wire the electron flow through an external circuit and you've built a battery. Push energy in backwards and you get electrolysis. Same chemistry, two directions.

🔌

CELL ANATOMY — THE RULES THAT NEVER BREAK (R3.2.5)

RED CAT: REDuction at CAThode, oxidation at anode — always, both cell types

  • Anode = oxidation (electrons leave). Cathode = reduction (electrons arrive).
  • Voltaic (primary) cell: spontaneous reaction → electricity. Zn anode (−) in ZnSO₄, Cu cathode (+) in CuSO₄, salt bridge + wire.
  • Salt bridge (e.g. KNO₃, Na₂SO₄): cations drift to the cathode side, anions to the anode side — keeps charges balanced so electrons keep flowing. Without it, polarization stops the cell.
  • Cell diagram: anode | anode ion || cathode ion | cathode. Daniell: Zn(s) | Zn²⁺(aq) || Cu²⁺(aq) | Cu(s). Salt bridge = the two parallel lines.
  • Electrolytic cell: electricity drives a NON-spontaneous reaction (electrolysis). Same anode/cathode rules.
R3.2.6 · SL

PRIMARY (VOLTAIC) CELL

Galvani (frog legs twitching) → Volta (first battery, the pile).

Daniell cell: Zn(s) → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ (anode, −) · Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu(s) (cathode, +).

As it runs: blue CuSO₄ fades, copper bar grows, zinc bar thins.

Irreversible — reactants consumed, battery dies.

R3.2.7 · SL

SECONDARY (RECHARGEABLE) CELLS

Reactions reversed by applying current.

Lead-acid: Pb + PbO₂ + 2H₂SO₄ ⇌ 2PbSO₄ + 2H₂O (discharge →, charge ←).

Lithium-ion: Li in graphite + LiCoO₂ cathode. Discharge: Li → Li⁺ + e⁻ · Li⁺ + e⁻ + CoO₂ → LiCoO₂. Non-aqueous gel — Li reacts violently with water.

Fuel cells: not rechargeable — need a steady fuel supply. 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O via PEM. DMFC (methanol) emits CO₂.

Figure 3 — Daniell cell (Fig 11). Electrons flow anode → cathode through the wire; ions flow through the salt bridge to keep both halves neutral.
WORKED EXAMPLE 6 (book p.597): Mn(s) + Ni²⁺(aq) → Mn²⁺(aq) + Ni(s). (b) Cathode: Ni²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Ni (reduction); anode: Mn → Mn²⁺ + 2e⁻ (oxidation). (c) Cell diagram: Mn(s) | Mn²⁺(aq) || Ni²⁺(aq) | Ni(s).
⚡

ELECTROLYSIS — PUSHING ELECTRONS UPHILL (R3.2.8)

electrolytic cell = energy in, non-spontaneous reaction out

  • Molten NaCl: cathode: Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na(l) · anode: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ · overall: 2NaCl(l) → 2Na(l) + Cl₂(g).
  • Reactive metals (Li, Mg, Al, Na) are ALL made by electrolysis of molten salts — in an inert atmosphere (they react with oxygen).
  • Molten PbBr₂: Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb(l) · 2Br⁻ → Br₂(g) + 2e⁻.
  • Electrons flow from the negative terminal → cathode (reduces cations there); anions go to the anode, get oxidized, electrons return to the positive terminal.
Act 4 · R3.2.9 · R3.2.10 · R3.2.11

ORGANIC REDOX 🧪

Carbon plays the same game: oxidation = gains oxygen / loses hydrogen (oxidation state of C increases). Alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, acids — a redox ladder.

R3.2.9 · SL

OXIDATION OF ALCOHOLS

Primary alcohol → aldehyde (distillation, alcohol in excess): R-CH₂OH --[O]--> R-CHO + H₂O

→ carboxylic acid (reflux, oxidizing agent in excess): R-CHO --[O]--> R-COOH

Secondary alcohol → ketone (reflux): R-CH(OH)-R' --[O]--> R-CO-R' + H₂O

Tertiary alcohols CANNOT be oxidized — no H on the C bearing the OH.

[O] = oxidizing agent (e.g. acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, orange → green).

R3.2.10 · SL

REDUCTION — THE WAY BACK

Ketone → secondary alcohol: R-CO-R' --[H]--> R-CH(OH)-R'

Carboxylic acid → aldehyde → primary alcohol (aldehyde usually not isolated): R-COOH --[H]--> R-CH₂OH

Reducing agents: LiAlH₄ (everything); NaBH₄ (aldehydes + ketones only, NOT carboxylic acids).

[H] = reducing agent.

R3.2.11 · SL

ALKENES & ALKYNES

Unsaturated = has C=C or C≡C. Reduction = add H₂, lower the degree of unsaturation.

Alkyne → alkene: R-C≡C-R' + H₂ → alkene (deactivated Pd catalyst).

Alkene → alkane: R-CH=CH-R'' + H₂ → R-CH₂-CH₂-R'' (Ni or Pt catalyst).

Alkyne → alkane: + 2H₂ with excess hydrogen.

Propene + H₂ → propane · pent-1-yne + 2H₂ → pentane.

REAL WORLD: Van Gogh's yellows faded because lead chromate (PbCrO₄) and chromium pigments undergo redox over time. Chemists + art historians + conservators = science as a shared endeavour.

THE VOLTAGE METER 📟

AHL: quantify electron greed. E° ranks every half-reaction on one number line. E°cell tells you which way is spontaneous, and ΔG° = −nFE°cell converts volts into energy.

⚖️

THE SHE — EVERYTHING MEASURED AGAINST ZERO (R3.2.12)

standard hydrogen electrode: H⁺(aq) + e⁻ ⇌ ½H₂(g), E° = 0.00 V

  • Conditions: H₂ at 1 bar, H⁺ at 1.0 mol dm⁻³, 298 K (SATP: 298 K, 100 kPa).
  • Inert platinum electrode — the reduced species is a gas, not a metal.
  • More negative E° = easier to oxidize (better reducing agent, higher in activity series). More positive E° = easier to reduce (better oxidizing agent).
  • Key values: Li⁺/Li −3.04 · Zn²⁺/Zn −0.76 · Fe²⁺/Fe −0.45 · H⁺/H₂ 0.00 · Cu²⁺/Cu +0.34 · Ag⁺/Ag +0.80 · F₂/F⁻ +2.87 (data booklet §19).
R3.2.13 · AHL

E°CELL = THE SPONTANEITY GAUGE

E°cell = E°(reduced) − E°(oxidized) = E°(cathode) − E°(anode)

Positive E°cell → spontaneous forward.

Fe/Cu: E°cell = (+0.34) − (−0.45) = +0.79 V → Fe + Cu²⁺ → Fe²⁺ + Cu is spontaneous.

Zn/SHE: 0.00 − (−0.76) = +0.76 V. Cu/Ag: 0.80 − 0.34 = +0.46 V.

More positive E° = cathode. Coefficients never multiply E° (WE10 note).

R3.2.14 · AHL

ΔG° = −nFE°CELL

n = electrons transferred · F = 9.65 × 10⁴ C mol⁻¹ (Faraday).

1 V = 1 J C⁻¹ → product units = J mol⁻¹ (÷1000 for kJ).

Zn/SHE: n = 2, E° = 0.76 → ΔG° = −2 × 9.65×10⁴ × 0.76 = −1.47 × 10⁵ J mol⁻¹ = −147 kJ mol⁻¹ → spontaneous ✓ (ΔG° < 0).

Practice: ΔG° = −152 kJ (Fe/Cu) → E°cell = 152000/(2 × 96500) = +0.79 V ✓.

💧

AQUEOUS ELECTROLYSIS — WATER FIGHTS BACK (R3.2.15)

compare every ion against water's own redox

  • Cathode competition: Na⁺/Na is −2.71 V, but water H₂O + e⁻ → ½H₂ + OH⁻ is −0.83 V. Less negative wins → H₂ evolves, Na⁺ stays in solution.
  • Anode competition: water oxidation H₂O → ½O₂ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ (−1.23 V) vs Cl⁻ → ½Cl₂ + e⁻ (−1.36 V). Water favoured — but in CONCENTRATED chloride, Cl₂ comes off (small margin).
  • Net for NaCl(aq): 2NaCl + 2H₂O → H₂ + Cl₂ + 2NaOH.
  • Sulfate can never be oxidized — S is already at +6 (max).
  • Molten vs aqueous NaCl = the classic comparison question.
🏆

ELECTROPLATING — THE GOLDEN COAT (R3.2.16)

metal anode → ions → thin layer on the cathode

  • CuSO₄ with copper electrodes: anode erodes (Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻), cathode gains copper (Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu). Blue colour NEVER fades — ions removed = ions replenished.
  • Copper purification: impure Cu = anode; pure Cu plates the cathode; impurities stay put or stay in solution.
  • Electroplating: steel object = cathode, CuSO₄ solution, copper anode → steel gets a copper coat.
  • Real world: the Golden Bear trophy (Berlin Film Festival) is gold-plated bronze.
Figure 4 — The electrochemical series (data booklet §19). Every half-reaction gets a number; the gap between two numbers predicts the cell.
WORKED EXAMPLES 9–11 (AHL, book p.610–614): (9) SHE + Zn half-cell: SHE = cathode (0.00 > −0.76) → E°cell = 0.00 − (−0.76) = +0.76 V. (10) Cu + Ag: Ag = cathode → E°cell = 0.80 − 0.34 = +0.46 V — and note the silver voltage is NOT doubled even though 2Ag⁺ is reduced. (11) For the Zn/SHE cell, n = 2: ΔG° = −2 × (9.65×10⁴) × 0.76 = −1.47 × 10⁵ J mol⁻¹ = −147 kJ mol⁻¹.

SELF-TEST ✍

No peeking at the acts above. Pick an answer, get instant feedback.

1. OIL RIG means oxidation is...

✅ Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons). Reduction Is Gain.
❌ Oxidation = Loss. Reduction Is Gain — that's the whole OIL RIG.

2. In 4KClO₃ → 3KClO₄ + KCl, chlorine is...

✅ Cl goes from +5 to +7 in KClO₄ (oxidized) and +5 to −1 in KCl (reduced) — the same species does both. Disproportionation.
❌ Some Cl is oxidized (+5 → +7), some reduced (+5 → −1). One species, both jobs.

3. In Fe + 2HBr → FeBr₂ + H₂, the oxidizing agent is...

✅ HBr gets reduced (H: +1 → 0) so HBr is the oxidizing agent. Fe is oxidized → reducing agent. Describe the compound, not the atom.
❌ HBr — the compound that gets reduced. (Fe is the reducing agent; it gets oxidized.)

4. Which halogen can oxidize Br⁻ to Br₂?

✅ F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂ — anything ABOVE bromine displaces it: Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂. I₂ is below → no reaction.
❌ Cl₂ (or F₂). Halogen reactivity increases UP the group; only stronger ones displace weaker ones.

5. Which metal does NOT react with dilute HCl?

✅ Cu is below H in the activity series — can't displace it. Mg and Zn are above H → salt + H₂.
❌ Copper — below hydrogen. The pop test stays silent.

6. RED CAT: reduction happens at the...

✅ REDuction at CAThode — in voltaic AND electrolytic cells. Oxidation is always at the anode.
❌ Cathode. (Anode = oxidation, always.)

7. Why does a voltaic cell need a salt bridge?

✅ Electrons leave Zn (slightly +) and pile up at Cu (slightly −); the salt bridge lets cations/anions drift to balance the charge. Without it: polarization kills the cell.
❌ It balances charge. Ions flow through it (not electrons) to prevent polarization.

8. In the Daniell cell Zn | Zn²⁺ || Cu²⁺ | Cu, the anode is...

✅ Zn is oxidized (loses e⁻) → anode, written on the LEFT of the diagram. Cu²⁺ is reduced at the cathode, written on the right.
❌ Zinc. Oxidation = anode = left side of the cell diagram.

9. Electrolysis of MOLTEN NaCl produces...

✅ Molten: only Na⁺ and Cl⁻ exist — Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na(l) at the cathode, 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ at the anode. (In AQUEOUS NaCl, water competes and H₂ comes off instead.)
❌ Na + Cl₂. Water isn't there to compete — this is how reactive metals are made.

10. Oxidation of a secondary alcohol gives a...

✅ Secondary R-CH(OH)-R' --[O]--> ketone R-CO-R'. (Primary → aldehyde → acid; tertiary: no reaction.)
❌ Ketone. Only primary alcohols can reach aldehydes/acids.

11. (SL) Which reducing agent CANNOT reduce a carboxylic acid?

✅ NaBH₄ reduces aldehydes and ketones only. LiAlH₄ is the one that can handle carboxylic acids → primary alcohols.
❌ NaBH₄ — too gentle for carboxylic acids. LiAlH₄ is the full-strength version.

12. (AHL) Zn²⁺/Zn = −0.76 V, Cu²⁺/Cu = +0.34 V. The spontaneous cell has E°cell =...

✅ E°cell = E°(cathode) − E°(anode) = 0.34 − (−0.76) = +1.10 V. Positive → spontaneous: Zn → Zn²⁺, Cu²⁺ → Cu.
❌ +1.10 V. Subtract the anode's value: 0.34 − (−0.76). (The 0.42 trap forgets the sign.)

13. (AHL) For a 2-electron cell with E°cell = +0.76 V, ΔG° is...

✅ ΔG° = −nFE° = −2 × 9.65×10⁴ × 0.76 = −1.47×10⁵ J mol⁻¹ = −147 kJ mol⁻¹. Negative ΔG° ↔ positive E°cell ↔ spontaneous.
❌ −147 kJ mol⁻¹. Watch the sign (negative for spontaneous) and units (J → kJ ÷ 1000).

14. (AHL) Electrolysis of AQUEOUS NaCl: what forms at the cathode?

✅ Water's reduction (−0.83 V) is less negative than Na⁺ (−2.71 V) → H₂ evolves, Na⁺ and OH⁻ stay in solution. Molten NaCl is the only way to sodium metal.
❌ H₂. Compare ions against water: −0.83 V beats −2.71 V every time.
