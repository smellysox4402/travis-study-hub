📖 Definitions bank

THE WORDS THAT SCORE

Exact definitions, one line each. If you can say these, you can start any "state" or "explain" question.

TermDefinition
Scientific notationWriting a number as a × 10k where 1 ≤ a < 10 and k is an integer. 4 300 000 = 4.3 × 106.
Arithmetic sequenceA list where each term differs from the next by a common difference d. 5, 8, 11, 14… (d = 3).
Geometric sequenceA list where each term is multiplied by a common ratio r to get the next. 8, 4, 2, 1… (r = ½).
SeriesThe sum of the terms of a sequence. "Sequence" = list, "series" = added together.
Compound interestInterest paid on the original amount and on interest already earned — money grows geometrically.
DepreciationValue decreasing by a fixed percentage each year — a geometric sequence with r < 1.
AnnuityA series of equal regular payments into or out of an account (saving up, or a pension).
AmortizationPaying off a loan with regular equal payments; each payment covers interest plus some principal.
LogarithmThe inverse of a power. log10 x = "the power you raise 10 to, to get x". log10 1000 = 3 because 10³ = 1000.
Upper / lower boundThe interval a rounded value must actually sit in. 6.7 (1 dp) lies in 6.65 ≤ x < 6.75.
Percentage error|measured − exact| ÷ exact × 100%. How far a measurement is off, as a % of the true value.

EXAM TRAP — Arithmetic vs geometric: if terms go up/down by a constant amount, it's arithmetic (use d). If they're multiplied by a constant factor, it's geometric (use r). The words "doubles", "halves", "5% each year" all scream geometric.
🔢 Key facts

NUMBERS TO HAVE LOADED

The small constants that save you a GDC trip in the exam.

  • e ≈ 2.71828 — the base of the natural log, ln. ln e = 1, ln 1 = 0.
  • log10 1 = 0 · log10 10 = 1 · log10 100 = 2 · log10 1000 = 3 · log10 10 000 = 4.
  • 100 = 1, and 10−k = 1/10k (10−3 = 0.001).
  • Scientific notation keeps 1 ≤ a < 10: 0.0045 = 4.5 × 10−3, not 45 × 10−4.
  • A number rounded to d decimal places is off by at most half a unit in the last place: 6.7 (1 dp) → bounds 6.65 and 6.75.
  • Annuity / amortization: use the GDC TVM solver — N, I%, PV, PMT, FV, P/Y, C/Y. PV is positive money in, PMT/FV signed by direction.
  • GDC solves systems of up to 3 linear equations (linSolve / rref) and polynomial equations (polyRoots / nSolve) — 1.8 is pure technology marks.
🎯 Worked examples

FIVE QUESTIONS, STEP BY STEP

Model answers with the marks in mind — and the trap that catches people out on each one.

1. Find the 10th term and the sum of the first 10 terms of the arithmetic sequence 5, 8, 11, …
d = 3. u10 = 5 + (10 − 1)(3) = 5 + 27 = 32. Then S10 = 10/2 (5 + 32) = 5 × 37 = 185.
Trap: "10th term" needs (n − 1) = 9 in the formula, not 10. Writing u10 = 5 + 10×3 is the classic −1 slip.
2. Evaluate the sum to infinity of 8 + 4 + 2 + 1 + …
Geometric, r = 4/8 = 0.5, and |r| < 1 so a sum to infinity exists. S∞ = 8/(1 − 0.5) = 8/0.5 = 16.
Trap: S∞ only exists when |r| < 1. If r ≥ 1 the series keeps growing and "sum to infinity" has no answer.
3. $2500 is invested at 3.5% per year, compounded annually. Find its value after 6 years.
FV = 2500(1.035)6 = $3073.14 (to the nearest cent).
Trap: i is the decimal rate 0.035, and n is the number of periods. Compounded monthly for 6 years would be i = 0.035/12 and n = 72.
4. A laptop bought for $1200 depreciates 15% per year. Find its value after 4 years.
FV = 1200(1 − 0.15)4 = 1200(0.85)4 = $626.41.
Trap: depreciation multiplies by (1 − r), not (1 + r). Using 1.15 gives a laptop that gains value.
5. A length is measured as 6.7 m correct to 1 decimal place. State the lower and upper bounds, and the maximum percentage error.
Bounds: 6.65 m ≤ L < 6.75 m. Max error = 0.05 m. % error = 0.05/6.7 × 100 ≈ 0.75%.
Trap: the upper bound is 6.75 with a strict "less than" (a value of exactly 6.75 would round to 6.8). Lower bound 6.65 is inclusive.
6. Solve 10x = 500.
x = log10 500 ≈ 2.699 (using the GDC log key).
Trap: log10 500 is not 50. It's the power, so it sits between 2 (=100) and 3 (=1000).
✏️ Self-test

QUICK QUIZ — 6 QUESTIONS

No notes, no GDC where it says so. Pick an answer and it tells you right away.

Q1. Find the 10th term of the arithmetic sequence 3, 7, 11, …

✅ 39. u10 = 3 + 9×4 = 39.
❌ Not quite. d = 4, so u10 = 3 + (10−1)×4 = 3 + 36 = 39.

Q2. The sum to infinity of 12 + 6 + 3 + … is:

✅ 24. r = ½, so S∞ = 12/(1 − ½) = 24.
❌ S∞ = u1/(1 − r) = 12/(1 − 0.5) = 24.

Q3. $2000 at 4% p.a. compounded annually for 5 years is worth:

✅ $2433.31. 2000(1.04)⁵ = 2433.31.
❌ 2000 × 1.04⁵ = 2000 × 1.21665 = $2433.31.

Q4. log10 10 000 = ?

✅ 4. 10⁴ = 10 000, so log10 10 000 = 4.
❌ The log is the power: 10 000 = 10⁴, so the answer is 4.

Q5. A value is given as 8.3 (correct to 1 dp). The lower bound is:

✅ 8.25. Rounding to 1 dp is off by at most 0.05.
❌ 1 dp → half of 0.1 is 0.05, so 8.3 − 0.05 = 8.25.

Q6. The sum of the first 8 terms of 2, 5, 8, … is:

✅ 100. S₈ = 8/2(2×2 + 7×3) = 4 × 25 = 100.
❌ d = 3, u₁ = 2: S₈ = 4(4 + 21) = 100.
