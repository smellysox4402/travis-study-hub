3.1

3D geometry

Distance and midpoint in 3D, and the volume / surface area of the solids that keep reappearing.

DISTANCE & MIDPOINT IN 3D

d = √((x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)² + (z₂−z₁)²)

Midpoint = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2, (z₁+z₂)/2) — just the average of each coordinate.

Trap: it's three differences. 3-4-12 → √(9+16+144) = 13 is a favourite.

3.2 · 3.3

Right-angle trigonometry

SOH CAH TOA and Pythagoras — the foundation everything else sits on.

📐

SOH CAH TOA

sin = opposite/hypotenuse · cos = adjacent/hypotenuse · tan = opposite/adjacent

  • Pythagoras: a² + b² = c² (c = hypotenuse, opposite the right angle).
  • Angle of elevation = up from the horizontal; depression = down from the horizontal.
  • To find an angle from a ratio, use the inverse: θ = sin⁻¹(O/H), etc.

Exact values worth knowing: sin 30° = ½, cos 60° = ½, tan 45° = 1, sin 90° = 1.

WORKED — elevation

A ladder 5 m long leans against a wall, its foot 3 m from the wall. Find the angle the ladder makes with the ground.

cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse = 3/5, so θ = cos⁻¹(0.6) = 53.1°.

📟 GDC — fx-CG50 (inverse trig)

  1. Make sure you're in degrees: SHIFT → MENU (SET UP) → Angle → F1 (Deg)
  2. SHIFT → cos (gives cos⁻¹) → type 0.6 → EXE → 53.13°
3.2 · 3.3

Sine & cosine rule

For triangles that aren't right-angled — which rule to reach for, and why.

SINE RULE

a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C

Use when you have a side and its opposite angle (plus one other side or angle).

⚠️ SSA can give two answers — the ambiguous case.

COSINE RULE

a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A

Use for two sides + the included angle, or all three sides.

Rearrange for an angle: cos A = (b² + c² − a²)/(2bc).

AREA

Area = ½ ab sin C

Two sides × the sine of the included angle.

WORKED — sine rule

In a triangle, A = 30°, a = 10, B = 45°. Find b.

b = a · sin B / sin A = 10 × sin 45° / sin 30° = 10 × 0.7071 / 0.5 = 14.14.

WORKED — cosine rule

Two sides are 5 and 7, with included angle 60°. Find the third side.

a² = 5² + 7² − 2(5)(7) cos 60° = 25 + 49 − 35 = 39, so a = √39 ≈ 6.24.

WHICH RULE? Two sides + included angle, or three sides → cosine. A side and its opposite angle → sine. The included-angle question is the one most often misread as sine rule.
3.4 · 3.5 · 3.6

Circles & Voronoi diagrams

Arcs and sectors (radians!), perpendicular bisectors, and the Voronoi diagram.

ARC LENGTH & SECTOR AREA

Arc length l = rθ · Sector area A = ½r²θ  (θ in radians)

  • Convert degrees → radians: multiply by π/180 (45° = π/4).
  • 360° = 2π, 180° = π, 90° = π/2.

📟 GDC — angle mode (THE circle trap)

  1. SHIFT → MENU (SET UP) → Angle
  2. F1 = Degrees · F2 = Radians

Arc/sector formulas need radians. If the GDC is in degrees, l = rθ with θ = 45 gives nonsense. Set radians for circles, degrees for triangles.

PERPENDICULAR BISECTORS (3.5)

A line that cuts a segment in half at a right angle. Every point on it is equidistant from the two endpoints — that's the property that builds Voronoi diagrams.

VORONOI DIAGRAMS (3.6)

Splits a plane into cells so every point belongs to the nearest site. The edges are perpendicular bisectors between neighbouring sites; where edges meet are vertices.

  • Nearest-neighbour interpolation: estimate an unknown value by taking the value of the site whose cell the point sits in.
  • Adding a site only redraws the cells around its immediate neighbours.
  • Toxic-waste dump problem: place the dump to maximise its distance from every site — the point furthest from all sites (a vertex of the diagram).

SELF-TEST ✍

No notes. Pick an answer, get instant feedback.

1. The distance from the origin to (3, 4, 12) is:

✅ 13. √(9 + 16 + 144) = √169 = 13.
❌ √(3² + 4² + 12²) = √169 = 13.

2. sin 30° = ?

✅ 0.5. sin 30° = ½.
❌ sin 30° = ½ (30-60-90 triangle).

3. The area of a triangle with sides 6 and 8 and included angle 30° is:

✅ 12. (1/2)(6)(8) sin 30° = 24 × 0.5 = 12.
❌ Area = ½ab sin C = ½(6)(8)(0.5) = 12.

4. The arc length of a radius-10 circle for a 90° angle is:

✅ 5π. 90° = π/2, so l = 10(π/2) = 5π.
❌ Convert first: 90° = π/2, then l = rθ = 10(π/2) = 5π.

5. The volume of a cone with radius 3 and height 4 is:

✅ 12π. V = ⅓πr²h = ⅓π(9)(4) = 12π.
❌ V = ⅓πr²h = ⅓π(3²)(4) = 12π.

6. The surface area of a sphere of radius 5 is:

✅ 100π. SA = 4πr² = 4π(25) = 100π.
❌ Surface area is 4πr² = 100π; (500/3)π is the volume.

🎯 EXAM WALKTHROUGHS

Q1 — CALCULATE [2 marks] "Find the distance between (1, 2, 3) and (4, 6, 15)."

Model answer: √(3² + 4² + 12²) = √169 = 13 (2 marks).

Q2 — CALCULATE [2 marks] "Two sides are 5 and 7 with included angle 60°. Find the third side."

Model answer: a² = 25 + 49 − 70 cos 60° = 39 → a = 6.24 (method 1, answer 1).

Trap: cosine rule needs the included angle. Not included → sine rule (and check for the ambiguous case).

Q3 — CALCULATE [2 marks] "A circle of radius 8 cm has a 45° sector. Find its area."

Model answer: θ = π/4 (1) · A = ½(8)²(π/4) = 8π ≈ 25.1 cm² (1).

Trap: sector area is ½r²θ, not r²θ — the missing ½ is the single most common slip.

Q4 — EXPLAIN [2 marks] "Why does the sine rule sometimes give two possible angles?"

Model answer: sin θ = sin(180° − θ) (1), so a given sine value corresponds to two possible angles, and the SSA arrangement can fit two different triangles (1).
