B4.2.1

THE ECOLOGICAL NICHE

A niche is the role of a species in an ecosystem, including all its biotic and abiotic interactions.

The ecological niche is the role of a species — how it lives, what it eats, where it lives, and its biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) interactions that affect its growth, survival and reproduction.

B4.2.2

OBLIGATE ANAEROBES, FACULTATIVE ANAEROBES & OBLIGATE AEROBES

Organisms differ in their tolerance to the presence of oxygen.

TypeOxygenExample
Obligate aerobeneeds O₂ (can't live without it)most animals, plants
Obligate anaerobeonly lives without O₂ (O₂ is toxic)some bacteria (e.g. Clostridium)
Facultative anaerobecan use O₂ but also live without ityeast, E. coli
B4.2.3

PHOTOSYNTHESIS AS NUTRITION

Photosynthesis is the mode of nutrition in plants, algae and several photosynthetic prokaryotes.

Autotrophic organisms make their own food. Photosynthesis is the mode of nutrition in plants, algae and several groups of photosynthetic prokaryotes (e.g. cyanobacteria) — they convert light into chemical energy.

B4.2.4

HOLOZOIC NUTRITION IN ANIMALS

All animals are heterotrophic. In holozoic nutrition, food is ingested, digested internally, absorbed and used.

Holozoic nutrition is the mode of heterotrophic nutrition in animals: food is ingested, digested internally (in a gut), absorbed and incorporated. All animals are heterotrophic (they can't make their own food).

B4.2.5

MIXOTROPHIC NUTRITION

Some protists are mixotrophic — both autotrophic and heterotrophic, e.g. Euglena.

Mixotrophic organisms can switch between autotrophic and heterotrophic nutrition. Euglena is a freshwater protist that photosynthesises in light but feeds heterotrophically in darkness. Many other protists are also mixotrophic.

B4.2.6

SAPROTROPHIC NUTRITION (DECOMPOSERS)

Some fungi and bacteria feed saprotrophically and are called decomposers.

Saprotrophic nutrition = feeding on dead or decaying organic matter by digesting it externally. Fungi and bacteria with this mode are called decomposers — they release enzymes onto the matter and absorb the products.

B4.2.7

DIVERSITY OF NUTRITION IN ARCHAEA

Archaea are one of the three domains of life and are metabolically very diverse.

Archaea are one of the three domains of life (with Bacteria and Eukarya). They are metabolically very diverse — including autotrophs, heterotrophs, and many that live in extreme environments (e.g. methanogens, halophiles).

B4.2.8

DENTITION & DIET IN THE HOMINIDAE

The dentition (teeth) of omnivorous and herbivorous members of the family Hominidae relates to their diet.

  • Omnivores (e.g. humans) — have a mix of incisors, canines (moderate), premolars and molars for both plant and animal food.
  • Herbivores (e.g. gorillas) — have large, flat molars for grinding plant material and prominent canines (used for display/defence).
B4.2.9

HERBIVORE ADAPTATIONS & PLANT DEFENCES

Herbivores have adaptations for feeding on plants; plants have adaptations to resist herbivory.

HERBIVORE

Feeding adaptations

e.g. piercing and chewing mouthparts of leaf-eating insects (like a locust); mammals with broad grinding molars and a long digestive tract.

PLANT

Anti-herbivory defences

Thorns/spines, tough leaves, chemical toxins (e.g. alkaloids), and tannins that make leaves unpalatable.

B4.2.10

PREDATOR & PREY ADAPTATIONS

Predators have adaptations for finding, catching and killing prey; prey have adaptations to resist predation.

PREDATOR

Finding, catching, killing

Sharp teeth/claws, speed, camouflage, sensory organs, venom.

PREY

Resisting predation

Chemical (toxins), physical (spines, shells, camouflage) and behavioural (speed, hiding, warning colouration) defences.

B4.2.11

PLANT FORM & HARVESTING LIGHT

Plants have adaptations of form to harvest light, with different strategies in forest ecosystems.

  • Tall emergent trees reach above the canopy (e.g. dipterocarps).
  • Canopy trees spread leaves to capture light.
  • Understorey/shrub species have large, broad leaves to capture the limited light reaching them, or shade-tolerant adaptations.
B4.2.12

FUNDAMENTAL & REALIZED NICHES

The fundamental niche is the potential range based on adaptations and tolerance; the realized niche is the actual range used.

  • Fundamental niche — the full potential niche a species could occupy, based on its adaptations and tolerance limits (ignoring competition).
  • Realized niche — the actual niche the species occupies, which is typically smaller because of competition, predation and other interactions.
FIG 1 — The fundamental niche (potential) is wider than the realised niche (actual), which is restricted by competition.
B4.2.13

COMPETITIVE EXCLUSION & NICHE UNIQUENESS

If two species compete for the same niche, one is eliminated or both are restricted to part of their fundamental niche.

Competitive exclusion — two species cannot occupy the same niche indefinitely: one outcompetes the other (which is eliminated), or both are restricted to a part of their fundamental niche. This is why every ecological niche is unique.
