A4.2.1

WHAT IS BIODIVERSITY

Biodiversity is the variety of life in all its forms, levels and combinations — ecosystem, species and genetic diversity.

  • Ecosystem diversity — the variety of different habitats, communities and ecosystems.
  • Species diversity — the number and variety of species.
  • Genetic diversity — the variety of genes within a species (variation between individuals).
A4.2.2

HOW MANY SPECIES?

Millions of species have been discovered and named, but there are many more still to be found.

Millions of species have been discovered, named and described, but the total number on Earth is far higher — many species are still undiscovered, especially in the oceans and rainforests. Estimates of the total run into the tens of millions.

A4.2.3

CAUSES OF ANTHROPOGENIC SPECIES EXTINCTION

The current sixth mass extinction is caused by human activity, unlike previous mass extinctions.

The present sixth mass extinction is driven by anthropogenic (human) causes — it is not a natural event like earlier mass extinctions. The extinction rate is far above the natural background rate.

A4.2.4

CAUSES OF ECOSYSTEM LOSS

Ecosystem loss is directly or indirectly caused by humans. Two case studies include the loss of mixed dipterocarp forest and another ecosystem.

  • Mixed dipterocarp forest (Southeast Asia) — logged and cleared for timber and palm-oil plantations, causing huge loss of forest ecosystems.
  • Another case of ecosystem loss (e.g. tropical rainforest / mangroves) — also removed by human activity.
  • Both losses reduce biodiversity and the services the ecosystems provide.
A4.2.5

EVIDENCE OF THE BIODIVERSITY CRISIS

Evidence comes from IPBES reports and reliable surveys showing declines in species and ecosystems.

The IPBES (Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services) reports summarise global evidence of an accelerating biodiversity crisis. Reliable surveys consistently show declining species populations, shrinking habitats and rising extinction risk.

A4.2.6

CAUSES OF THE CURRENT BIODIVERSITY CRISIS

Human population growth is the overarching cause, together with over-exploitation, urbanization, deforestation and more.

  • Human population growth — the overarching driver (more people → more demand).
  • Hunting and over-exploitation — taking species faster than they can recover.
  • Urbanization — building over habitats.
  • Deforestation and habitat destruction.
  • Pollution, invasive species, climate change — additional pressures.
A4.2.7

SEVERAL APPROACHES ARE NEEDED TO CONSERVE BIODIVERSITY

No single approach is enough; different species need different measures — including in situ and ex situ conservation.

IN SITU

In its natural habitat

Protecting species where they live — e.g. national parks, nature reserves, protected areas, marine protected areas, and preserving whole ecosystems.

EX SITU

Outside its habitat

Protecting species away from the wild — e.g. zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks, captive breeding programmes.

Key point: different species need different approaches, so a combination (in situ + ex situ + legislation + protected areas) is required.
A4.2.8

THE EDGE OF EXISTENCE PROGRAMME

The EDGE programme prioritises species that are both evolutionarily distinct and globally endangered.

EDGE (Evolutionarily Distinct and Globally Endangered) focuses conservation on species that are evolutionarily distinct (with few close relatives, so they represent a lot of unique evolutionary history) and globally endangered. Protecting these species preserves more genetic/evolutionary diversity per unit of effort.
