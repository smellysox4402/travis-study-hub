D1.1.1

WHY CELLS REPLICATE DNA

DNA replication makes exact copies with identical base sequences — needed for reproduction and growth.

DNA replication produces exact copies of DNA with identical base sequences. It is required for reproduction (passing DNA to offspring) and growth (making new cells by mitosis).

D1.1.2

SEMI-CONSERVATIVE REPLICATION

Replication is semi-conservative: each new DNA has one old and one new strand, using complementary base pairing for accuracy.

In semi-conservative replication, the double helix unwinds and each old strand acts as a template for a new complementary strand. Because of complementary base pairing (A–T, G–C), the copies are highly accurate.

FIG 1 — Semi-conservative replication: each new double helix contains one old (template) strand and one newly synthesised strand.
D1.1.3

HELICASE & DNA POLYMERASE

Helicase unwinds the DNA and breaks hydrogen bonds; DNA polymerase makes the new strand.

  • Helicase — unwinds the double helix and breaks the hydrogen bonds between strands.
  • DNA polymerase — adds nucleotides to build the new complementary strand (template-directed).
D1.1.4

PCR & GEL ELECTROPHORESIS

PCR amplifies DNA; gel electrophoresis separates DNA fragments. PCR uses primers, temperature changes and Taq polymerase.

PCR

Amplify DNA

Uses primers, repeated temperature changes (to denature, anneal and extend), and Taq polymerase (heat-stable). Makes many copies of a DNA segment.

ELECTROPHORESIS

Separate DNA

DNA fragments are separated by size as they move through a gel under an electric field — smaller fragments move further.

D1.1.5

APPLICATIONS OF PCR & ELECTROPHORESIS

PCR and gel electrophoresis have broad applications, including DNA profiling (paternity, forensics).

  • DNA profiling — for paternity testing and forensics (identifying DNA from a crime scene).
  • Other applications: detecting genetic diseases, studying populations, identifying species, sequencing DNA.
AHL · D1.1.6

DIRECTIONALITY OF DNA POLYMERASES

DNA polymerases add nucleotides to the 3′ end of the growing strand.

DNA strands have a 5′ end and a 3′ end. DNA polymerase reads the template 3′→5′ and adds new nucleotides to the 3′ end of the new strand — so the new strand grows 5′→3′.

AHL · D1.1.7

LEADING vs LAGGING STRAND

One strand is made continuously (leading), the other discontinuously (lagging, in Okazaki fragments).

  • Leading strand — synthesised continuously towards the fork (5′→3′).
  • Lagging strand — synthesised discontinuously as short Okazaki fragments (made away from the fork).
FIG 2 — The replication fork: the leading strand is made continuously; the lagging strand is made in Okazaki fragments that ligase joins.
AHL · D1.1.8

THE ENZYMES OF REPLICATION

Primase, DNA polymerase I, DNA polymerase III and DNA ligase (prokaryotic system).

EnzymeRole
DNA primaselays down a short RNA primer to start synthesis.
DNA polymerase IIIthe main replicating enzyme (adds nucleotides to the new strands).
DNA polymerase Iremoves the RNA primers and replaces them with DNA.
DNA ligasejoins the Okazaki fragments / seals nicks in the sugar-phosphate backbone.
AHL · D1.1.9

DNA PROOFREADING

DNA polymerase III proofreads, removing a mismatched nucleotide from the 3′ end and replacing it.

DNA polymerase III has proofreading ability: it can detect a mismatched base at the 3′ terminal, remove it, and replace it with the correct nucleotide. This greatly reduces errors during replication.
