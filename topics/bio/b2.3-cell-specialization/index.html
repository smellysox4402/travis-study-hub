B2.3.1

DIFFERENTIATION

Fertilisation produces unspecialised cells that develop into specialised cells by differentiation, influenced by gradients on gene expression.

After fertilisation, an embryo's cells are initially unspecialised; they then become specialised through differentiation. Gradients of chemicals in the early embryo affect gene expression, so different cells switch on different genes and take on different roles.

B2.3.2

PROPERTIES OF STEM CELLS

Stem cells can divide endlessly and differentiate along different pathways.

A stem cell has two key properties: it can divide endlessly (self-renew) and it can differentiate along different pathways to form specialised cells.

B2.3.3

STEM CELL NICHES IN ADULT HUMANS

Stem cell niches maintain stem cells or promote their proliferation and differentiation. Two example locations.

A stem cell niche is a local environment where stem cells are maintained, or where they are stimulated to proliferate and differentiate. Two adult human examples: bone marrow (blood stem cells) and hair follicles (skin stem cells).

B2.3.4

TOTIPOTENT vs PLURIPOTENT vs MULTIPOTENT

Cells in early embryos are totipotent but soon become pluripotent; adult stem cells (e.g. in bone marrow) are multipotent.

TypeCan formExample
TOTIPOTENTall cell types incl. extra-embryonic (a whole organism)early embryo cells (zygote, very early)
PLURIPOTENTall body cell types (but not extra-embryonic)later embryo cells
MULTIPOTENTseveral related cell typesadult bone-marrow stem cells

B2.3.5

CELL SIZE AS AN ASPECT OF SPECIALIZATION

Human cells vary widely in size: gametes, red and white blood cells, neurons and striated muscle fibres.

  • Gametes — the egg is one of the largest human cells; the sperm is among the smallest.
  • Red blood cells — small (~7 µm).
  • White blood cells — larger than RBCs.
  • Neurons — very long (e.g. from spinal cord to toes).
  • Striated muscle fibres — very long, multinucleate cells.
B2.3.6

SURFACE AREA TO VOLUME RATIO & CELL SIZE

As a cell grows, its surface area-to-volume ratio falls; exchange depends on surface area, demand on volume.

Materials cross a cell's surface but are used throughout its volume. As a cell gets bigger, volume grows faster than surface area, so the SA:vol ratio decreases. This limits cell size — beyond a point there isn't enough surface to supply the volume.

AHL · B2.3.7

INCREASING SURFACE AREA : VOLUME

Cells can increase their SA:vol by flattening, microvilli and invagination. Examples: erythrocytes and proximal convoluted tubule cells.

  • Flattening — a flat cell has a high SA:vol (e.g. erythrocytes — biconcave discs).
  • Microvilli — tiny folds that greatly increase surface area (e.g. proximal convoluted tubule cells in the nephron, and the small intestine).
  • Invagination — folding inward increases internal surface.
AHL · B2.3.8

TYPE I & TYPE II PNEUMOCYTES

Type I pneumocytes are extremely thin (short diffusion distance); type II pneumocytes have lamellar bodies that secrete surfactant.

  • Type I pneumocytes — extremely thin to reduce the diffusion distance for gas exchange.
  • Type II pneumocytes — have many secretory vesicles (lamellar bodies) that discharge surfactant into the alveolus, stopping it from collapsing.
Note: the alveolar epithelium has more than one cell type because different adaptations are needed for the overall function of the tissue.
AHL · B2.3.9

CARDIAC MUSCLE vs STRIATED MUSCLE

Both have contractile myofibrils, but differ in branching, length and number of nuclei.

FeatureCardiac muscleStriated (skeletal) muscle
Myofibrilspresentpresent
Branchingbranched (network)unbranched (parallel)
Nucleioften one, centralmany, peripheral (multinucleate)
Lengthshorter cellsvery long fibres
Discussion: whether a striated muscle fibre is a single cell (it is often treated as one, very long, multinucleate cell).
AHL · B2.3.10

ADAPTATIONS OF SPERM & EGG CELLS

Human gametes are adapted to their roles in fertilisation.

SPERM

Small, motile

Flagellum for swimming, many mitochondria for ATP, an acrosome with enzymes to digest the egg's outer layers, streamlined and haploid.

EGG

Large, food-filled

A large cytoplasm with yolk for nutrition, a zona pellucida + corona for protection/recognition, and two polar bodies discarded (haploid).
