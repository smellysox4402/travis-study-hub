D1.3.1

GENE MUTATIONS

Gene mutations are structural changes to genes at the molecular level: substitutions, insertions and deletions.

A gene mutation is a structural change to a gene at the molecular level. Three types: substitution (one base swapped for another), insertion (bases added), and deletion (bases removed).

FIG 1 — Gene mutations: substitution changes one base; insertion/deletion shifts the reading frame (frameshift).
D1.3.2

CONSEQUENCES OF BASE SUBSTITUTIONS

Substitutions can cause single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs); because of degeneracy, often no amino acid changes.

  • A base substitution changes one base; the result is a single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP).
  • Because the genetic code is degenerate, a substitution may not change the amino acid (silent), or may change one amino acid.
D1.3.3

CONSEQUENCES OF INSERTIONS & DELETIONS

Insertions and deletions often cause frameshift changes, so the polypeptide ceases to function.

Because the code is read in triplets, an insertion or deletion shifts the reading frame (frameshift mutation) — scrambling all downstream codons. This usually produces a non-functional polypeptide, or a truncated one.

D1.3.4

CAUSES OF GENE MUTATIONS

Mutations are caused by mutagens and by errors in DNA replication or repair.

  • Mutagens — e.g. chemical mutagens, UV and ionising radiation.
  • Errors in DNA replication and errors in DNA repair.
D1.3.5

RANDOMNESS OF MUTATION

Mutations can occur anywhere in the genome, though some bases have a higher probability of mutating.

Mutations are random — they can occur anywhere in the base sequences of the genome. However, some bases have a higher probability of mutating (hypermutable sites, e.g. repetitive sequences, CpG sites).

D1.3.6

GERM CELLS vs SOMATIC CELLS

Mutations in germ cells are inherited; mutations in somatic cells can cause cancer.

  • Germ-cell mutations — occur in gametes, so the mutated gene is inherited by offspring.
  • Somatic-cell mutations — occur in body cells; not inherited, but can cause cancer (e.g. from a mutation in a proto-oncogene/tumour-suppressor gene).
D1.3.7

MUTATION AS A SOURCE OF VARIATION

Gene mutation is the original source of all genetic variation.

Gene mutation is the original source of all genetic variation. Most mutations are neutral or harmful; only a small minority are advantageous. But without mutation, there would be no variation for selection to act on.

AHL · D1.3.8

GENE KNOCKOUT

Gene knockout investigates a gene's function by changing it to make it inoperative.

Gene knockout is a technique that alters a gene to make it inoperative. By observing what the organism loses, scientists can infer the gene's function.

AHL · D1.3.9

CRISPR-Cas9 GENE EDITING

CRISPR sequences and the enzyme Cas9 are used in gene editing.

CRISPR sequences (with a guide RNA) direct the enzyme Cas9 to a specific DNA sequence, where it cuts the DNA. This allows a gene to be edited (removed, replaced or modified) — a precise gene-editing tool.

AHL · D1.3.10

CONSERVED & HIGHLY CONSERVED SEQUENCES

Conserved sequences are similar across species; hypotheses account for them.

  • Conserved sequences — identical or similar across a species or group; highly conserved = almost unchanged across long evolutionary times.
  • These genes have essential functions (e.g. ribosomal RNA, histone, haemoglobin genes) — their sequences are kept stable by selection (because mutations are usually harmful).
