D3.2.1

THE GUEST LIST

How traits actually get passed on: half your tickets from mum, half from dad, one full set each.

🎟️

Half from mum, half from dad

gametes · haploid · zygote · meiosis

In a sexual life cycle, parents pass genes to offspring in gametes — egg and sperm. Gametes are haploid (n): they contain one chromosome of each type, whether male or female. So both parents make an equal genetic contribution.

When the gametes fuse, the nuclei join and the chromosome number doubles: the zygote is diploid (2n), with two chromosomes of each type. The halving trick that makes this possible is meiosis — one diploid nucleus divides twice to make four haploid nuclei.

Figure 1 · The sexual life cycle: meiosis halves 2n → n in gametes; fertilisation restores 2n in the zygote. Equal tickets from both parents.
Memory: gametes are the haploid tickets (one of each chromosome), zygote is the diploid pair. If gametes weren't halved, the chromosome number would double every generation — the doubling trap.
THE HALVING

Meiosis

A diploid nucleus divides twice to produce four haploid nuclei — one copy of each gene per gamete.

THE DOUBLING

Fertilisation

Egg + sperm nuclei join → chromosome number doubles back to diploid. n + n = 2n. That's inheritance's whole trick.

D3.2.2

HOW MENDEL RAN THE CLUB

The controlled-cross method: how a monk in a garden figured out inheritance before DNA was even a word.

🧪

The original bouncer: Gregor Mendel

P generation · F1 · F2 · controlled crosses

Patterns of inheritance are investigated by crossing varieties — transferring pollen from the anthers of one plant (the male parent) onto the stigma of another (the female parent). A paintbrush works, or dab an anther directly on the stigma.

To keep the cross honest, all other pollen must be blocked: cut off the flower's own anthers before they mature, then enclose it in a paper bag so insects and wind can't sneak pollen in. Self-pollination (pollen from the same plant) would wreck the experiment.

The known parents are the P generation; offspring in the seeds are the F₁ (first filial); their offspring are F₂. Mendel crossed tall × dwarf peas, purple × white flowers, counted every offspring, and calculated ratios — unheard of in the 19th century.

Figure 2 · The controlled cross: block stray pollen, dust donor pollen on, let the seeds tell the story. Ratios are the whole game.
Self-pollination = self-fertilisation. It happens when pollen from a plant lands on its own stigma — no cross between varieties happens, and the experiment is void. Remove the anthers before they ripen and bag the flower.
