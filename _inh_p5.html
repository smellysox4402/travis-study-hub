60-second recap

THE CHEAT ⚡

🎟️ Gametes are haploid (n); fusion makes the diploid (2n) zygote — meiosis halves, fertilisation restores.
🎫 Alleles = versions of a gene. Genotype = allele combo (DD/Dd/dd); phenotype = what shows (genotype × environment).
💪 Dominant allele masks recessive → 3:1 from Tt × Tt. One functional copy is enough.
🦎 Phenotypic plasticity = same genes, expression switched — reversible, not mutation.
🧬 PKU: two carriers → 25% affected child (aa). Autosomal, chromosome 12, preventable by diet.
🩸 ABO = 3 alleles, 4 types · IᴬIᴮ codominant (both show) · O universal donor.
⚧ Sex: mother X × father X or Y → 1:1 · SRY on Y = testis-determining factor.
🩹 Haemophilia = X-linked recessive → mostly boys · carrier daughters 50%, affected sons 50%.
🎲 Dihybrid RrYy × RrYy → 9:3:3:1 · segregation + independent assortment.
📊 Continuous = polygenic + environment → box-and-whisker (min, Q1, median, Q3, max, IQR, outliers).
🔗 Linkage: genes on one chromosome travel together → parentals ≫ recombinants · χ² = Σ(obs−exp)²/exp, df = classes − 1, critical 7.815 at 3 df.
Bouncer quiz

THE TEST ✍️

10 questions. Pick an answer — instant feedback. No mercy from the bouncer.

1. Gametes are…

✅ Gametes are haploid (n): one of each chromosome. Meiosis halves 2n → n; fertilisation restores 2n.
❌ Gametes are haploid — the halving that makes n + n = 2n possible. Meiosis does the halving.

2. Why bag the flower during a controlled cross?

✅ The bag keeps the cross honest: only your chosen donor pollen reaches the stigma.
❌ The bag blocks stray pollen. Anthers are cut off first so the flower can't self-pollinate.

3. A heterozygous individual has genotype…

✅ Heterozygous = Dd: two different alleles, split 50/50 into gametes.
❌ Same alleles = homozygous (DD or dd). And you only carry two alleles of most genes, not three.

4. Phenotypic plasticity means…

✅ Plasticity = gene expression changes with environment; the genes themselves stay identical and it's reversible.
❌ No mutation, no Lamarckian inheritance. Only gene expression changes — tanning is the classic example.

5. Two PKU carriers (Aa × Aa) have a…

✅ Aa × Aa → AA : Aa : Aa : aa = 25% affected. Both carriers are symptom-free, so it arrives as a surprise.
❌ The ratio is 1:2:1 — only the aa child (1 in 4) develops the disease.

6. Blood group AB is an example of…

✅ IᴬIᴮ shows both glycoproteins — codominance, not a blend. (Blending = incomplete dominance, like pink Mirabilis flowers.)
❌ AB is codominant: both alleles fully expressed at once. Incomplete dominance would give an intermediate phenotype.

7. What determines the sex of a human baby?

✅ Eggs always carry X; sperm carry X or Y. The sperm decides — and it's the PRESENCE of Y (SRY gene) that makes a male.
❌ Mother contributes X only. XXY (Klinefelter) is male despite two Xs — the Y is what matters.

8. Why is haemophilia far more common in boys?

✅ X-linked recessive: a boy's single X has no second copy to cover the disease allele. Girls with one healthy X are carriers.
❌ The gene is on the X (Factor VIII), and the allele is recessive — a boy's one X is exposed, so it shows.

9. RrYy × RrYy gives the phenotype ratio…

✅ Two unlinked heterozygous genes → 9:3:3:1. Independent assortment gives four equally likely gametes per parent.
❌ 9:3:3:1 is the signature of two independently assorting genes. (Mostly-parental = linkage.)

10. In a chi-squared test on 4 phenotype classes (df = 3), the critical value at p = 0.05 is…

✅ At 3 df, p = 0.05, critical χ² = 7.815. Above it → significant deviation (e.g. linkage). Below → keep the null hypothesis.
❌ 3.841 is the df = 1 value. For 4 classes, df = 4 − 1 = 3, critical value 7.815.

  • ✓D3.2.1Haploid gametes (n) fuse to form the diploid zygote (2n). Meiosis halves the chromosome number; fertilisation restores it. Parents make equal genetic contributions.
  • ✓D3.2.2Controlled crosses in flowering plants: transfer pollen anther → stigma, cut off own anthers, bag the flower to block stray pollen. P → F₁ → F₂ generations; ratios reveal patterns.
  • ✓D3.2.3-5Alleles = versions of a gene; genotype = allele combination (homozygous/heterozygous); phenotype = observable traits (genotype × environment). Dominant masks recessive → Tt × Tt gives 3:1; one functional copy is enough.
  • ✓D3.2.6Phenotypic plasticity: same genotype, environment flips gene expression (tanning, dark-grown seedlings). Reversible — genes switched on/off, not mutated.
  • ✓D3.2.7PKU: recessive allele of phenylalanine hydroxylase gene (chr 12, autosomal). Carriers (Aa) symptom-free; Aa × Aa → 25% affected (aa). Phenylalanine builds up → brain damage; screening + low-phenylalanine diet prevents it.
  • ✓D3.2.8-10Gene pools hold multiple alleles; SNPs = single-base variations. ABO: Iᴬ, Iᴮ, i → 4 blood types; IᴬIᴮ codominant (both show). Incomplete dominance = intermediate (pink Mirabilis). O = universal donor, AB = universal recipient.
  • ✓D3.2.11Sex determination: XX female, XY male; sperm decides (X → daughter, Y → son), 1:1. SRY gene on Y = testis-determining factor. XXY = male (Klinefelter), XO = female (Turner) — Y presence decides.
  • ✓D3.2.12-13Haemophilia: X-linked recessive (Factor VIII on X). Boys affected (one X), girls carriers (two Xs); carrier mother × normal father → sons 50% affected, daughters 50% carriers. Pedigrees: squares/circles, filled = affected; unaffected parents + affected child = recessive.
  • ✓D3.2.14-17Continuous variation = polygenic + environment (box-and-whisker: min, Q1, median, Q3, max, IQR, outliers); discrete = few genes. Segregation + independent assortment → dihybrid 9:3:3:1; split into two 3:1 crosses to check.
  • ✓D3.2.18-21AHL: loci = gene positions (~20,000 genes). Linked genes on one chromosome travel together → parentals ≫ recombinants (crossing over). χ² = Σ(obs−exp)²/exp, df = classes − 1, critical 7.815 at 3 df, p = 0.05 — above = reject H₀.
